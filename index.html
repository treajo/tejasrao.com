How it scores

A guide starts at 100 and loses points for the things that usually cost you an experiment: GC content outside 40 to 60 percent, a TTTT run that terminates Pol III transcription, a G or C homopolymer of four or more, and no G at the 5′ end for U6 transcription. It is a rule-of-thumb filter, not an on-target efficiency model, and it says nothing about off-targets. For real work, put your pick through a proper design tool.

Researchers write in about everything from shipping dates to a gene fragment that would not assemble. The routine questions were taking the team's day, but an agent that guesses about biology would cost more trust than it saves time.

What I did

  • Read through months of past conversations and sorted them into safe for AI, handle with care, and always a scientist.
  • Rewrote the help content so every answer had one clear source to cite.
  • Wrote the handoff rules: anything tied to a specific order, sequence or failed experiment goes to a person, with the conversation summarised.
  • Sampled answers weekly, tagged each miss by cause, and fixed the cause rather than the symptom.

What stuck

  • Most of what looked like model failure was missing or messy content.
  • A clean handoff is a feature. People trust an agent that knows where its knowledge ends.
It got better every time I fixed a document, not a prompt.
